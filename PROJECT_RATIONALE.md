Project Rationale — Why Mach1 Exists
Background

Mach1 started from a simple observation: most public-sector financial analysis focuses on how much is spent, but far less attention is paid to how dependent the system is on specific vendors.

In large institutions, risk does not usually come from a lack of diversification on paper, but from hidden structural dependencies that only become visible when something breaks.

The Question I Wanted to Answer

Rather than asking “Where is spending highest?”, I framed the problem differently:

Where is public-sector spending quietly dependent on a small number of vendors in a way that could create operational or fiscal risk if disrupted?

This framing mattered because:

High spend alone does not imply risk

Broad vendor lists can mask real dependency

Risk often emerges from concentration combined with institutional reach

Why Vendor Dependency

I deliberately focused on vendor dependency because it sits at the intersection of:

Finance and risk management

Operations and service delivery

Procurement and institutional resilience

If a vendor receives a large share of payments and is embedded across multiple ministries, disruption can cascade quickly across government functions.

That is the kind of risk leaders want surfaced early, not after a crisis.

Why Public Data

I chose to work exclusively with public payment data to:

Prove that meaningful risk signals can be extracted without proprietary information

Avoid assumptions tied to confidential contracts or internal systems

Keep the project transparent, auditable, and defensible

The constraint of public data forced clarity and discipline in how the analysis was designed.

Design Principles

From the start, Mach1 followed four principles:

Explainability over complexity
Every metric and threshold must be understandable without technical explanation.

Risk discovery, not prediction
The goal is to surface where attention is warranted, not to forecast outcomes.

Minimal scope, maximum signal
The MVP answers one question well instead of many questions poorly.

Decision-readiness
Outputs should be consumable by a finance professional in minutes.

What Mach1 Is — and Is Not

Mach1 is:

A risk discovery MVP

A prioritization tool

A structured way to surface non-obvious dependency risk

Mach1 is not:

A dashboard-first project

A forecasting engine

A policy recommendation system

This distinction was intentional.

Why This Matters

Most institutional failures are not caused by unknown risks, but by known risks that were never surfaced clearly enough to act on.

Mach1 exists to reduce that blind spot.
