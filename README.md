Mach1
Public-Sector Vendor Dependency Risk Analytics (MVP)
Overview

Mach1 is a risk analytics MVP designed to surface hidden vendor concentration and dependency risks in public-sector spending using publicly available data. The project identifies vendors whose scale and cross-ministry presence create potential structural single points of failure that may not be visible in standard budget or expenditure reports.

Problem

Public-sector spending often appears diversified across thousands of vendors. In practice, risk can accumulate when a small number of vendors receive a disproportionate share of payments and are embedded across multiple ministries, creating fragile dependencies.

Solution

Mach1 analyzes public payment data to:

Measure vendor spending concentration

Map vendor presence across government ministries

Identify vendors combining scale and institutional breadth

Classify vendors into clear, explainable risk tiers

The output is a short, decision-ready list of dependency risks.

Data

Source: Ontario Public Accounts — Payments

Fiscal Year: 2024–2025

Data Type: Public, non-confidential transaction records

Methodology (High Level)

Aggregate payments by vendor to calculate concentration

Identify vendors operating across multiple ministries

Combine concentration and ministry breadth into a dependency signal

Classify vendors into High, Medium, and Monitor risk tiers

Key Findings (MVP)

Over 10,000 unique vendors analyzed

1,563 vendors operate across multiple ministries

Only 5 vendors combine high concentration and multi-ministry presence

These vendors represent elevated structural dependency risk

Outputs

Vendor concentration tables

Dependency-ranked vendor list

Risk tier classification

Executive summary for senior decision-makers

Limitations

Mach1 identifies risk signals, not failures. It does not assess contract terms, service criticality, or contingency planning.

Conclusion — What Mach1 Reveals

The Mach1 MVP shows that while Ontario public-sector spending is distributed across more than 10,000 vendors, structural dependency risk is highly concentrated.

High-Risk Vendor Dependencies

Mach1 identified five vendors that meet both conditions for elevated structural risk:

A large share of total government spending

Presence across multiple ministries, creating cross-institutional dependency

These vendors include major public-sector and quasi-public entities such as:

Accounts Under $120,000 / Comptes Inférieurs À 120 000$, which aggregates a large volume of smaller but system-wide payments spanning 26 ministries

City of Toronto, with significant payment concentration and exposure across 12 ministries

Ontario Health / Santé Ontario, a critical health-sector body embedded across 5 ministries

Metrolinx, a major transportation agency with cross-ministry funding exposure

Toronto District School Board, representing education-sector dependency across multiple ministries

These entities collectively account for a disproportionate share of total spending and are embedded across key government functions such as health, transportation, education, and municipal operations.

Why This Matters

From a finance or risk perspective, these vendors represent potential single points of failure:

Disruption affecting one of these entities could have cascading operational and fiscal impacts across multiple ministries.

Risk is not limited to vendor size alone, but to how widely embedded the vendor is within the institutional structure.

Traditional expenditure reports do not surface this type of dependency clearly.

Mach1 demonstrates that dependency risk is structural, not merely financial.

Ministries Most Exposed to Dependency Risk

The analysis also shows that dependency risk clusters around ministries associated with:

Health and healthcare delivery

Transportation and infrastructure

Education and municipal transfers

These ministries rely on a smaller number of large, systemically embedded vendors, increasing exposure to coordination, continuity, and contingency risk.

Key Takeaway for Decision-Makers

The primary insight from Mach1 is not that spending is “too concentrated,” but that a small number of vendors quietly sit at the intersection of scale and institutional reach.

This makes them:

Priority candidates for enhanced oversight

Logical starting points for contingency planning

Appropriate subjects for deeper contractual and operational review

Mach1 does not recommend action — it prioritizes attention.

Final Perspective

Mach1 shows that even in highly distributed public-sector systems, risk concentrates in predictable but non-obvious places. By reframing the question from “how much is spent” to “where the system is structurally dependent,” the MVP provides a practical lens for early risk discovery.

This project demonstrates how disciplined analytics, clear framing, and public data can support better risk awareness before dependency becomes crisis.
