Mach1
Public-Sector Vendor Dependency Risk Analytics (MVP)
Overview

Mach1 is a risk analytics MVP designed to surface hidden vendor concentration and dependency risks in public-sector spending using publicly available data. The project identifies vendors whose scale and cross-ministry presence create potential structural single points of failure that may not be visible in standard budget or expenditure reports.

Problem

Public-sector spending often appears diversified across thousands of vendors. In practice, risk can accumulate when a small number of vendors receive a disproportionate share of payments and are embedded across multiple ministries, creating fragile dependencies.

Solution

Mach1 analyzes public payment data to:

Measure vendor spending concentration

Map vendor presence across government ministries

Identify vendors combining scale and institutional breadth

Classify vendors into clear, explainable risk tiers

The output is a short, decision-ready list of dependency risks.

Data

Source: Ontario Public Accounts — Payments

Fiscal Year: 2024–2025

Data Type: Public, non-confidential transaction records

Methodology (High Level)

Aggregate payments by vendor to calculate concentration

Identify vendors operating across multiple ministries

Combine concentration and ministry breadth into a dependency signal

Classify vendors into High, Medium, and Monitor risk tiers

Key Findings (MVP)

Over 10,000 unique vendors analyzed

1,563 vendors operate across multiple ministries

Only 5 vendors combine high concentration and multi-ministry presence

These vendors represent elevated structural dependency risk

Outputs

Vendor concentration tables

Dependency-ranked vendor list

Risk tier classification

Executive summary for senior decision-makers

Limitations

Mach1 identifies risk signals, not failures. It does not assess contract terms, service criticality, or contingency planning.
